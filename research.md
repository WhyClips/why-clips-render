# Research — "The First Spam Email Made $13 Million" (Why Clips)

## Story (verified)
- On **3 May 1978**, Gary Thuerk, a marketing manager at Digital Equipment
  Corporation (DEC), sent ONE email to about 400 people on ARPANET —
  the military/research network that became the internet — advertising
  DEC's new computers. It was the first unsolicited mass email in history:
  the first spam.
- ARPANET users were furious; the network was for scientists and soldiers,
  not salesmen. Thuerk's boss made him promise never to do it again.
- Twist: it WORKED. About 40 people came to the demos, and Thuerk claims
  the single email sold $12–13 million worth of computers.
- Guinness World Records lists it as the world's oldest spam. Thuerk is
  still proud of it and calls himself the pioneer of email marketing.

## Detail decisions
- Send date: **3 May 1978** (Computerworld's 1 May is the composition date).
- Recipient count varies by source (393 / 397 / ~400) — script says
  "about 400". Guinness's exact figure is 397, sent 12:33 EDT.
- Sales figure: Thuerk's own claim, "$12–13 million" — script attributes
  it ("Gary says..."), title uses $13 million.
- "No Gmail. No smartphones. Just giant humming computers in cold rooms."
  is scene-setting colour, not a factual claim.
