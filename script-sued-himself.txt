In 1995, a prisoner in Virginia sued the one man who ruined his life — himself. And he demanded five million dollars.

Robert Lee Brock was serving twenty-three years for breaking and entering and grand larceny. He decided someone had to pay. So he took the only man he could blame to court — Robert Lee Brock. No lawyer. No help. Just a pen, seven pages, and a wild idea.

In a seven-page handwritten letter, he laid out his case. On the first of July, nineteen ninety-three, he had drunk alcohol — breaking his own religious beliefs and his own civil rights. That drink, he said, led straight to his arrest and his prison cell.

So he demanded five million dollars. From himself.

You see, there was one problem. Brock had no money. No job. No income in prison. So he asked the state of Virginia to pay the five million on his behalf — and promised to pay it all back after his release.

The judge read the whole case. Judge Rebecca Beach Smith threw it out, calling it totally ludicrous. But then she praised him — for an innovative approach to civil rights litigation.
