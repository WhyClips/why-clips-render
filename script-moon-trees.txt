Five hundred seeds flew around the Moon in 1971 — NASA came home believing they were all dead. Today, one stands at the White House.

Apollo 14 launched. While two astronauts walked on the Moon, Stuart Roosa circled above them, alone — a former forest firefighter, carrying a tiny canister.

But the seeds never touched the Moon — they looped around it thirty-four times while the astronauts took the glory. Then splashdown shattered everything.

The canisters burst open. Five hundred seeds spilled out and mixed like salt — ruined, impossible to sort. Everyone wrote them off as dead.

But someone planted them anyway. In Mississippi and California, four hundred and twenty seedlings pushed through the soil, stubborn as weeds after a wildfire.

For America's two hundredth birthday, those trees went to fifty states. First in the ground: Philadelphia, May 1975. One went to the White House.

Then they were forgotten — until a schoolteacher asked NASA: where are they? You see, one scientist hunted them from newspaper clippings. A hundred and ten found.

In twenty twenty-two, NASA did it again — Artemis One carried a thousand new seeds around the Moon. Their grandchildren are growing now, maybe in a park near you.
