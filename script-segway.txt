In 2010, the owner of the Segway company died riding a Segway. Off a cliff. Into a river. And the worst part — he was only reversing to be polite.

Jimi Heselden was a coal miner's son. His Hesco blast walls — steel cages filled with sand — shield soldiers at war. He was worth a hundred and sixty-six million pounds. Camp Bastion was named after his firm.

On Christmas Day 2009, he bought the Segway company — the machine promised to be bigger than the internet. He owned it just nine months.

On September 26th, 2010, he rode his off-road Segway above the River Wharfe, walking his dog. Then a dog walker came down the same narrow path.

Jimi reversed to let him pass. The Segway wobbled at the cliff edge — and he went over. Forty-two feet down into the river. Dead at the scene, aged sixty-two.

Accidental death, said the inquest. The Segway was examined — no fault found. You see, the machine meant to change the world killed its owner — and it wasn't its fault.

He bought the company that promised to change how we move — and nine months later, it moved him off a cliff. The coroner called it an act of courtesy.
