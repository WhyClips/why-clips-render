In 1573, an English pirate stood on a hill in Panama and saw two oceans at once — because the men who led him there were runaway slaves.

Spain's silver crossed Panama on mule trains — tons of it. The men who carried it kept running away. Thousands of enslaved Africans fled into the jungle, built free villages. They called themselves cimarrones — the wild ones.

Spain sent armies to drag them back in chains. The cimarrones melted into the trees — and kept raiding the treasure road.

In 1572, Francis Drake came for Spain's silver. But Drake didn't know the jungle. The jungle knew him. The cimarrones found HIM. Their guide Pedro Mandinga offered a deal: we guide you, you share the silver.

In February 1573, thirty cimarrones led Drake across the isthmus. They ambushed a mule train, grabbed the silver, and hid it in a riverbed.

You see, Drake didn't discover the route. The men Spain enslaved knew it better than Spain did.

On the way, they climbed a hill and showed Drake what no Englishman had seen — the Pacific Ocean, from the same spot as the Atlantic. The slaves who owned nothing gave a pirate the world.
